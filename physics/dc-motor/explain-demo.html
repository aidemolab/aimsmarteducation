Showing the field round a wire

Push a straight wire up through a flat card and stand small plotting compasses on the card around it. With no current, every needle points north.

Switch on a large current for a few seconds: the needles swing round to line up in circles round the wire. Reverse the current and every needle turns round. Switch off and they point north again.

To draw the field, mark where each needle points, join the marks into circles and add arrows. Iron filings tapped onto the card show the circles too, but not their direction.

Safety: a large current makes the wire hot, so switch on only briefly.

Quick check: why use compasses rather than iron filings to show the field's direction?

Show the answer

Each compass needle points along the field, so it shows the direction. Filings only show the shape.

Electromagnets in devices

To explain a device from its diagram, follow four steps:

  1. Find the electromagnet and what switches its current on.
  2. Find the iron part it attracts when the current flows.
  3. Say what that movement does: closes a switch, strikes a gong, opens a lock.
  4. Say what happens when the current stops: usually a spring pulls the iron back.

Relay: a small current in the coil attracts an iron armature, which closes the contacts of a second circuit. A small, safe current can switch on a large one, such as a car's starter motor.

Quick check: in a relay, what pulls the armature back when the current stops?

Show the answer

A spring. With no current the coil is no longer a magnet, so nothing holds the armature.

Physics only: in GCSE Physics, not Combined Science. Practise on the electric bell in Do it, part 2.

Analogy: a stream Draft for Alaa's review

The compasses are like leaves floating on the water: each one lines up with the swirl where it sits.
