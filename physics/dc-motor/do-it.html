Part 5 of 7 The equation F = B I l

HT Fleming's left-hand rule, the motor and F = B I l Optional for Foundation

HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Equations and units

Force on a wire in a magnetic field

F = B I l

force = magnetic flux density × current × length. The wire must be at right angles to the field. A wire parallel to the field feels no force.

Quantities in F = B I l, their symbols and units
QuantitySymbolUnit
ForceFnewton (N)
Magnetic flux densityBtesla (T)
CurrentIampere (A)
Length of wire in the fieldlmetre (m)

Rearranging: B = F ÷ (I l) · I = F ÷ (B l) · l = F ÷ (B I)

Units first: change centimetres to metres (÷ 100), millimetres to metres (÷ 1000) and millinewtons to newtons (÷ 1000) before you substitute.

Part 6 of 7 Practice ladder: L1 to L4

HT Fleming's left-hand rule, the motor and F = B I l Optional for Foundation

Practice ladder: recall to new situations

  1. L1HT AQA 4.7.2.2 In Fleming's left-hand rule, what does each finger represent?

    Show the answer

    First finger: field. Second finger: current. Thumb: motion (force).

  2. L2HT AQA 4.7.2.3 Name the part of a DC motor that reverses the current in the coil every half turn. Going further

    Show the answer

    The split-ring commutator.

  3. L3HT AQA 4.7.2.3 Give three ways to make a DC motor spin faster.

    Show the answer

    Increase the current; use a stronger magnetic field; use more turns on the coil.

HT AQA 4.7.2.2 L4 · Worked example

5.0 cm of wire carries 3.0 A at right angles to a 0.20 T field. Calculate the force.

Convert the length to metres: l = 5.0 cm = 0.050 m

Write the equation: F = B I l

Substitute: F = 0.20 × 3.0 × 0.050

F = 0.030 N

  1. L8HT AQA 4.7.2.4 (Physics only) A loudspeaker has a coil in a magnetic field, attached to a cone. Explain why the cone moves back and forth when an alternating current flows. Going further

    Show the answer

    The current in the coil, in a magnetic field, gives a force (the motor effect). The current keeps reversing, so the force keeps reversing, and the cone moves back and forth.
