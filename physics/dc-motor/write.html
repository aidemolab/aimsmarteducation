Q1 · DescribeDescribe the magnetic field pattern around a long straight wire carrying a current. [2 marks]

Show the mark scheme
  • Circles (concentric) around the wire (1)
  • Field weaker / lines further apart further from the wire (1)

Examiner tip: draw circles centred on the wire, not lines spreading out from it.

All AQA 4.7.2.1

Q2 · GiveGive two ways to increase the strength of the magnetic field of a solenoid. [2 marks]

Show the mark scheme
  • Any two from: increase the current (1); add an iron core (1); more turns (in the same length) (1)

Examiner tip: "reverse the current" scores nothing: it swaps the poles but does not change the strength.

Part 2 of 6 Exam questions: Higher

HT Motor exam questions Optional for Foundation

HT AQA 4.7.2.3

Q4 · StateGive two ways to reverse the direction of rotation of a DC motor. [2 marks]

Show the mark scheme
  • Reverse the direction of the current (swap the supply connections) (1)
  • Reverse the magnetic field (swap the poles of the magnets) (1)

Examiner tip: doing both at once does not reverse it. This is a common wrong answer.

HT AQA 4.7.2.2

Q5 · CalculateA 12 cm length of wire at right angles to a magnetic field of flux density 0.25 T feels a force of 0.090 N. Calculate the current in the wire. [3 marks]

A
Show the mark scheme
  • Converts length: l = 0.12 m (1)
  • Rearranges and substitutes: I = 0.090 ÷ (0.25 × 0.12) (1)
  • I = 3.0 A (1)

Examiner tip: most lost marks come from leaving the length in centimetres.

HT AQA 4.7.2.3

HT AQA 4.7.2.3

Q7 · Extended responseExplain how a simple DC motor works, and describe how its speed of rotation could be increased. [6 marks]

Show the levels and indicative content
LevelMarksDescriptor
35–6A clear, logically linked explanation of the force on the coil, the turning effect and the role of the commutator, plus at least two valid ways to increase speed
23–4An explanation with some links, covering the force and turning, with at least one way to increase speed
11–2Isolated relevant points, for example "current in a magnetic field gives a force"
00Nothing worthy of credit

Indicative content: current in coil within magnetic field · force on each side (motor effect) · Fleming's left-hand rule gives direction · forces opposite on the two sides · turning effect · commutator reverses current every half turn · continuous rotation in one direction · speed increased by larger current, stronger field, more turns on the coil.

Examiner tip: the highest level needs a linked sequence (because… so…), not a list of facts. Build the chain trains exactly this.

Part 3 of 6 Knowledge organiser

Knowledge organiser

All AQA 4.7.2.1

Field around a wire
A current makes a magnetic field: circles centred on the wire. Stronger with more current, weaker further away. Reverse the current, reverse the field.
Solenoid
A coil of wire. Field inside strong and uniform; outside shaped like a bar magnet's field.
Electromagnet
A solenoid with an iron core. Stronger with more current, more turns or an iron core. Can be switched on and off.

HT AQA 4.7.2.2, 4.7.2.3

Motor effect
A wire carrying a current in a magnetic field experiences a force.
Direction
Fleming's left-hand rule: first finger field, second finger current, thumb motion (force).
Size
F = B I l: F in newtons, B in tesla, I in amperes, l in metres; wire at right angles to the field.
DC motor
Forces on the two sides of the coil act in opposite directions, so the coil turns.
Commutator Going further
Reverses the current in the coil every half turn, so rotation continues in one direction.
Faster / reverse
Faster: larger current, stronger field, more turns. Reverse: reverse the current or the field (not both).

Part 5 of 6 Exit check

Exit check

  1. All AQA 4.7.2.1 What shape is the magnetic field around a straight wire?

    Show the answer

    Circles centred on the wire.

  2. All AQA 4.7.2.1 What happens to the field if the current is reversed?

    Show the answer

    It reverses direction.

  3. All AQA 4.7.2.1 What is an electromagnet?

    Show the answer

    A solenoid with an iron core.

  4. HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Which rule gives the direction of the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field?

    Show the answer

    Fleming's left-hand rule.

  5. HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Calculate the force on 0.20 m of wire carrying 5.0 A at right angles to a 0.10 T field.

    Show the answer

    F = 0.10 × 5.0 × 0.20 = 0.10 N

  6. HT AQA 4.7.2.3 Why does a DC motor need a split-ring commutator? Going further

    Show the answer

    It reverses the current every half turn, so the coil keeps turning in one direction.

Day 3

All AQA 4.7.2.1

  1. What shape is the field around a straight wire?
  2. Where is that field strongest?
  3. What does an iron core do to a solenoid's field?

HT AQA 4.7.2.2, 4.7.2.3

  1. What is the motor effect?
  2. What does the thumb show in Fleming's left-hand rule?
  3. Why does a DC motor need a commutator?
Show the answers

All: circles centred on the wire · closest to the wire · makes it much stronger.

HT: a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force · the motion (force) · it reverses the current every half turn so the coil keeps turning one way.

Day 7

All AQA 4.7.2.1

  1. Describe the field inside a solenoid.
  2. Give two ways to make an electromagnet stronger.
  3. What happens to the poles if you reverse the current?

HT AQA 4.7.2.2, 4.7.2.3

  1. Give two ways to reverse a motor.
  2. Give two ways to make it faster.
  3. F = B I l: what is the unit of each quantity?
Show the answers

All: strong and uniform · more current, iron core, more turns (any two) · they swap ends.

HT: reverse the current; reverse the field · larger current, stronger field, more turns (any two) · F newtons, B tesla, I amperes, l metres.

Day 21

All AQA 4.7.2.1

  1. Sketch the field around a wire and around a solenoid from memory, then check.
  2. Why does a compass near a wire turn when the current is switched on?
  3. Why is an electromagnet, not a permanent magnet, used in a scrapyard crane?

HT AQA 4.7.2.2, 4.7.2.3

  1. Explain why the two sides of the coil move in opposite directions.
  2. Calculate F for 0.050 m, 2.0 A and 0.30 T.
  3. Why would a motor without a commutator stop at the vertical?
Show the answers

All: compare with the 3D model · the current makes a magnetic field, and the compass points along it · it can be switched off to drop the load.

HT: the current flows in opposite directions in the two sides, so the forces are opposite · F = 0.30 × 2.0 × 0.050 = 0.030 N · past the vertical the forces turn it back, so it rocks and settles there.
