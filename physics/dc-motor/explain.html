Foundation and Higher: the magnetic effect of a current

All AQA 4.7.2.1 Step A

A current makes a magnetic field

When a current flows through a wire, there is a magnetic field around it. The field lines are circles centred on the wire.

The field is stronger with a bigger current, and stronger closer to the wire. Reverse the current and the field reverses direction.

To find the field's direction, use a compass, or the right-hand grip rule: point your right thumb along the current, and your fingers curl the way the field goes. Always draw arrows on field lines.

Quick check: what happens to the field if the current is reversed?

Show the answer

The field reverses direction. Its shape stays the same: circles round the wire.

All AQA 4.7.2.1 Step B

Coil it up: the solenoid

Shaping the wire into a coil, a solenoid, makes the field stronger. Inside a solenoid the field is strong and uniform. Outside, it has the same shape as the field of a bar magnet.

Adding an iron core makes the field stronger still. A solenoid with an iron core is an electromagnet.

Quick check: give two ways to make a solenoid's field stronger.

Show the answer

Any two: increase the current; add an iron core; add more turns of wire in the same length.

Where you'll find this

  • All AQA 4.7.2.1 Electromagnets: scrapyard cranes switch theirs off to drop the scrap. Electric door locks and relays use small ones.
  • HT AQA 4.7.2.3 DC motors: electric toothbrushes, drones, car windows and computer fans all spin because of the motor effect.

Part 2 of 3 The force on the coil

HT How the motor works: five steps Optional for Foundation

HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Step 1

The magnetic field

Two magnets face each other. Between them, the field lines run from N to S across the gap.

Quick check: which way do the field lines point?

Show the answer

From N to S.

HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Step 2

A current in the coil

The supply drives a current through the brushes, the commutator and the coil.

Quick check: where does the current come from?

Show the answer

The power supply (a cell or battery).

HT AQA 4.7.2.2 Step 3

A force on each side

The two sides that cut across the field each feel a force: the motor effect. Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction: first finger field, second finger current, thumb motion.

Quick check: which sides of the coil feel a force?

Show the answer

The two sides that cut across the field lines. Sides parallel to the field feel no force.

Part 3 of 3 How the coil keeps turning

HT How the motor works: five steps Optional for Foundation

HT AQA 4.7.2.3 Step 4

The coil turns

The forces on the two sides act in opposite directions, so together they turn the coil.

Quick check: why do the two forces make the coil turn?

Show the answer

They act in opposite directions on opposite sides, giving a turning effect.

Pause and explain ✦ AI tutor

  1. Hint 1Trace the current around the coil.
  2. Hint 2Which way does it flow in each side?
  3. Model pointsThe current flows in opposite directions in the two sides, so the forces on them are opposite.

HT AQA 4.7.2.3 Step 5 Going further

The commutator keeps it turning

Every half turn, the split-ring commutator reverses the current in the coil. That keeps the forces turning the coil the same way.

Quick check: what does the commutator do every half turn?

Show the answer

It reverses the direction of the current in the coil.

The AQA specification asks you to explain why the coil rotates, but does not name the commutator.
