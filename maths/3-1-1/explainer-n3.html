Which step comes first? The order of operations

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn the agreed order for working out an expression.

With that order, everyone gets the same answer.

Here's a puzzle.

Two people work out 3 + 4 × 5.

One person gets 35.

The other gets 23.

Which one would you pick? Hold on to your answer.

They can't both be right.

So maths needs one agreed order of operations.

Your calculator uses that order, and so does every exam marker.

You already know the four operations from N2.

You know that 4 × 5 means four lots of five.

You also know how to place a value as a pin on our Zoomable Number Scale.

Today, each part of an expression will shrink into one value card.

At the end, the whole expression lands on one pin.

Let's go back to the puzzle.

4 × 5 means four lots of five, so it is one bundle.

We box the bundle, because it acts as one input.

We work the bundle out first, so it shrinks to the value card 20.

Then 3 + 20 is 23.

So 23 is right.

Working left to right gives 7 × 5, which is 35.

That splits the bundle apart, so it lands on the wrong pin.

Brackets tell you which part acts as one input.

In (3 + 4) × 5, the bracket makes 3 + 4 one input, so we work it out first: 7.

Then 7 × 5 is 35.

Moving the brackets moved the pin, and that's why brackets come first.

A power like 4² is short for 4 × 4.

So a power is a bundle of multiplications, and it is worked out before the multiplying around it.

A root undoes a power, so roots are worked out at the same stage as powers.

In 2 + √9 × 3, the root comes first: √9 is 3.

Next we multiply, because multiplying comes before adding: 3 × 3 is 9.

Last we add: 2 + 9 is 11.

Multiplication and division have equal priority.

That's because dividing by 4 is the same as multiplying by one quarter.

So when they sit side by side, we work from left to right.

24 ÷ 4 is 6, and then 6 × 2 is 12.

Doing the multiplication first would give 24 ÷ 8, which is 3, and that's wrong.

Addition and subtraction also have equal priority.

That's because taking away 3 is the same as adding negative 3.

So again, we work from left to right.

10 − 3 is 7, and then 7 + 2 is 9.

Doing the addition first would give 10 − 5, which is 5, and that's wrong.

If you've heard of BIDMAS, take care.

It does not mean addition before subtraction.

And it does not mean division before multiplication.

Now a trap.

Pause point. Pause the video now. What is −3²? Lock in one card: 9, −9, 6 or −6. Then press play.

Let's slow down here, because this is the key step.

The square acts only on the 3.

That's because the power is worked out before the minus sign is applied.

So −3² means the negative of 3², which is −9.

If you locked 6 or −6, you multiplied by 2, but squaring means 3 × 3.

To square negative three, you need brackets: (−3)² is −3 × −3, which is 9.

The two pins sit on opposite sides of zero, so the brackets really matter.

A fraction bar groups things too.

It acts like a bracket round the whole top and a bracket round the whole bottom.

So the top, 8 + 4, is 12.

The bottom, 5 − 2, is 3.

And 12 divided by 3 is 4.

So here's the whole order.

First, brackets and other groupings, like a fraction bar.

Second, powers and roots.

Third, multiply and divide, from left to right.

Fourth, add and subtract, from left to right.

Let's work out 20 − 2 × (3 + 1)² ÷ 4.

Step one: brackets first, because the bracket acts as one input.

3 + 1 is 4.

Pause point. Pause the video now. The line now reads 20 − 2 × 4² ÷ 4. Choose the next step, and justify why it comes before the others. Then press play.

Step two: the power comes next.

That's because 4² is a bundle of multiplications, so it must become one number before the 2 or the 4 can use it.

4² is 4 × 4, which is 16.

Step three: multiply and divide, from left to right, because they have equal priority.

2 × 16 is 32.

32 ÷ 4 is 8.

Step four: subtract last, because subtraction has the lowest priority here.

20 − 8 is 12.

So the answer is 12.

If you had subtracted first, you would get 18 × 16 ÷ 4, which is 72.

That lands on a very different pin, so the order really changes the value.

You've learned to evaluate an expression using the conventional priority of operations.

Brackets and fraction bars come first, then powers and roots.

Then multiply and divide, left to right.

Then add and subtract, left to right.

Follow that order, and everyone's pin lands in the same place.
