Q4 · Sort into groups (shown as multiple choice: pick the correct sorting)

Ella chooses teams of 2 from Amir (A), Beth (B), Cal (C), Dina (D) and Ezra (E). The order inside a team does not matter. Her cards: AB, AC, AD, AE, BA, BC, BD, BE, CD, CE, DE, ED, CB. Which cards are repeats, and how many different teams are there?

Goal N5.3 (Higher tier): Count combined choices using the product rule

Higher tier only. Foundation students can skip Q5 and Q6 and do Q7 and Q8 instead.

HT Q5 (predict) · Predict, then check

A four-digit PIN uses the digits 0 to 9. First predict: if digits may NOT be repeated, will there be about half as many PINs as when repeats are allowed, or far fewer than half?

HT Q5 (check) · Predict, then check

Now check: how many four-digit PINs are there if digits may NOT be repeated? Type the number without spaces.

HT Q6 · Open-ended (shown as a numeric answer, with an example explanation)

How many even four-digit numbers can be made from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, if no digit is used more than once? Work it out on paper and explain why you fill the units slot first, then type the number.

Example answer

Example (a fixed example, not marked by a computer): Units slot first, because it must be even: 3 choices (2, 4, 6). Then thousands: 5 choices, hundreds: 4, tens: 3. 3 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 180.
