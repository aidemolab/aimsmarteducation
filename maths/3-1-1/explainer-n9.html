Transcript

Writing numbers in standard form, and back again

Goal: You will learn to convert a number between ordinary form and standard form.

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn to change a number into standard form, and back again.

Here is a puzzle.

A learner wants to write four hundred and fifty thousand in standard form.

They see four zeros.

So they write four point five times ten to the power four.

Pick the card you think is right, and hold it.

We will test it later.

Very big and very small numbers are easy to misread.

One missing zero makes a number ten times too small.

Standard form writes every number in the same short way, so you can see its size at a glance.

You already know that ten to the power five is one hundred thousand.

You know that ten to the power nought is one.

And you know that ten to the power minus two means one over one hundred.

You also know that multiplying by ten moves every digit one column to the left.

We will use that fact to count the power.

Let's put four hundred and fifty thousand on our scale as a pin.

The pin shows the value.

The card under it shows how we write the value.

Standard form only rewrites the card, so it must never move the pin.

A number in standard form is A times ten to the power n.

A is called the coefficient.

The coefficient must be at least one and less than ten.

That rule gives every number exactly one standard-form card.

The power n is a whole number, so it can be positive, zero or negative.

Step one: we put a decimal point straight after the first non-zero digit.

That gives four point five.

Four point five is between one and ten, so it can be A.

Now we slow down, because this is the key step.

Step two: we count how many columns the digits move to get from four point five back to four hundred and fifty thousand.

We count columns because each move of one column is one times ten.

One. Two. Three. Four. Five.

The digits move five columns.

So the card reads four point five times ten to the power five.

Pause point.

Look again at the card from the puzzle: four point five times ten to the power four.

Where do you think that pin lands?

Choose one: four thousand five hundred; forty-five thousand; four hundred and fifty thousand; or four million five hundred thousand.

Lock in your choice.

Four point five times ten to the power four is forty-five thousand.

That pin is ten times smaller, so it is a different number.

Changing the index alone moves the pin.

The index counts the columns the digits move.

It does not count the zeros you can see.

Now let's zoom in, to a very small number: nought point nought three.

The first non-zero digit is the three, so A is three.

To get from three back to nought point nought three, the digits move two columns right.

Each move right is one divide by ten.

Dividing by ten twice is times ten to the power minus two.

So nought point nought three is three times ten to the power minus two.

Look at where the pin is.

It is to the right of zero, so the number is positive.

A negative power gives a small number, not a negative number.

Going back to ordinary form undoes the same moves.

Seven point nought two times ten to the power three has a positive power.

So we move the digits three columns left, which gives seven thousand and twenty.

Eight point three times ten to the power nought has a power of nought.

Ten to the power nought is one, so nothing moves: it is just eight point three.

Second pause point.

Here are three cards that all sit on the same pin.

Twenty-four times ten to the power five.

Two point four times ten to the power six.

Nought point two four times ten to the power seven.

Which one is in standard form?

Choose it, and be ready to say why.

All three cards have the same value, so the pin does not move.

Only two point four has a coefficient between one and ten.

Twenty-four is too big.

Nought point two four is too small.

Write nought point nought nought nought five nought seven in standard form. Then check it by changing it back.

First, we find the first non-zero digit.

It is the five, so we put the decimal point straight after it.

That gives five point nought seven.

Five point nought seven is between one and ten, so it can be A.

Next, we count the columns the digits move to get back to our number.

The number is small, so the digits move right.

They move four columns.

Four moves right is dividing by ten four times, which is times ten to the power minus four.

So the answer is five point nought seven times ten to the power minus four.

Now we check it by going back.

The power is minus four, so we move the digits of five point nought seven four columns right.

That gives nought point nought nought nought five nought seven.

It is the number we started with, so the pin has not moved and our card is right.

You can now convert a number between ordinary form and standard form.

First, put the decimal point after the first non-zero digit, so A is between one and ten.

Then count the columns the digits move: that count is the power of ten.

Big numbers have a positive power.

Small numbers have a negative power, and they are still positive.

To go back, move the digits the same number of columns the other way.

And the puzzle: four hundred and fifty thousand is four point five times ten to the power five, because the digits move five columns.
