Exam-style questions: N3 only, page 2

Parts labelled “N2 retrieval (not marked)” bring back earlier work and carry no marks. Original questions in the style of an AQA GCSE paper. Do them without a calculator. M marks are for a correct method, A marks for a correct answer after a correct method, B marks for a correct answer or statement on its own.

Q5(a) · N2 retrieval (not marked) · Numeric answer

(a) Work out 0.8 × 0.6

Mark scheme

Not marked: this part brings back N2.

Q5(b) · Spot the error in a worked solution · [2 marks]

(b) Priya works out 4.8 ÷ 0.6 like this. Describe Priya's error and work out the correct answer.

Line 1: 4.8 ÷ 0.6
Line 2: = 4.8 ÷ 6
Line 3: = 0.8

Mark scheme

B1 for saying both numbers must be multiplied by 10 (or that she changed the divisor only).

B1 for 8.

Saying Line 2 is wrong with no reason can still earn the B1 for 8, but not the B1 for describing the error.

Q6 · Numeric answer · [3 marks]

Without using a calculator, work out (48 × 25) ÷ (16 × 15). You must show your working.

Mark scheme

M1 for cancelling one common factor correctly, for example 48 ÷ 16 = 3 and 16 ÷ 16 = 1.

M1 for a second correct cancellation, for example cancelling 5 from 25 and 15 to give 5 and 3.

A1 for 5: (3 × 5) ÷ (1 × 3) = 5.

Long way: M1 for 48 × 25 = 1200 or 16 × 15 = 240; M1 for both products correct and an attempt at 1200 ÷ 240; A1 for 5.

Another route: cancel 16 to get 3 on top and 1 on the bottom, then cancel 3 from the 3 and the 15 (15 ÷ 3 = 5), leaving 25 ÷ 5 = 5. Same marks.

Q7 · True or false with a reason · [2 marks]

Tom says the fraction with top 6 + 9 and bottom 3 is equal to 9. His working: (6 + 9) over 3 = 6 + 3 = 9. Is Tom correct?

Mark scheme

B1 for “No” or “False” with the check 9 × 3 = 27, which is not 6 + 9 = 15.

B1 for 5.

“False” with no inverse check loses the first B1, because the question asks you to use an inverse operation.
