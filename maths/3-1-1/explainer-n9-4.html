Transcript

Adding and subtracting in standard form

Goal: You will learn to add or subtract numbers in standard form by first matching their powers of ten.

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn to add or subtract two numbers in standard form, by matching their powers of ten first.

Here is a puzzle.

What is three times ten to the power four, add two times ten to the power five?

Pick the card you think is right, and hold it.

Adding looks as easy as multiplying.

But it follows a different rule, and this is where many marks are lost.

You already know that you can only add like things.

Three tens add two tens is five tens.

But three tens add two hundreds is not five of anything.

You also know that one card can be rewritten without moving its pin.

For example, twenty times ten to the power nine is two times ten to the power ten.

Let's start with ordinary numbers, because we already trust them.

Three times ten to the power four is thirty thousand.

Two times ten to the power five is two hundred thousand.

Thirty thousand add two hundred thousand is two hundred and thirty thousand.

On the scale, the two jumps go end to end and land at two hundred and thirty thousand.

In standard form, that is two point three times ten to the power five.

So two point three times ten to the power five was the right card.

Five times ten to the power nine is five thousand million, which is far too big.

That card came from adding the coefficients and adding the indices.

But we are adding, not multiplying, so the index laws do not apply.

Now we slow down, because this is the key step.

The power of ten tells us the size of each step.

Ten to the power four is steps of ten thousand.

Ten to the power five is steps of one hundred thousand.

So we have three steps of one size and two steps of another size.

We cannot just add three and two, because the steps are not the same size.

Pause point.

To make the steps the same size, we rewrite two times ten to the power five using ten to the power four.

Which card has the same value:

nought point two times ten to the power four;

two times ten to the power four;

twenty times ten to the power four;

or two hundred times ten to the power four?

Lock in your choice.

Two hundred thousand is twenty lots of ten thousand.

So two times ten to the power five is twenty times ten to the power four.

We made the coefficient ten times bigger, so the power went down by one.

The pin did not move.

So the rule is: rewrite the number with the larger power into the smaller power.

Now both numbers count in steps of ten to the power four.

Three steps add twenty steps is twenty-three steps.

So the total is twenty-three times ten to the power four.

Twenty-three is not less than ten, so we rewrite the card once more.

It is two point three times ten to the power five, which matches our ordinary-number answer.

Subtracting works the same way, and so do small numbers.

Take five times ten to the power minus three, take away four times ten to the power minus four.

Minus three is the larger power, so we rewrite five times ten to the power minus three.

It becomes fifty times ten to the power minus four, because a coefficient ten times bigger needs a power one lower.

Fifty take away four is forty-six, so we get forty-six times ten to the power minus four.

That rewrites as four point six times ten to the power minus three.

We can check with ordinary numbers.

Nought point nought nought five take away nought point nought nought nought four is nought point nought nought four six.

That is four point six times ten to the power minus three.

Second pause point.

Why must the powers of ten match before we add or subtract?

Choose the best reason:

because the coefficients then count steps of the same size;

because the calculator needs it;

or because the answer must have the same power.

Hold your answer; we will come back to it in the recap.

Work out six point two times ten to the power six, take away eight times ten to the power five. Give your answer in standard form.

First, we look at the powers.

One is ten to the power six and the other is ten to the power five, so they do not match.

We rewrite the number with the larger power into the smaller power.

So six point two times ten to the power six will use ten to the power five.

We make the coefficient ten times bigger, which gives sixty-two.

So the power goes down by one, to five.

Six point two times ten to the power six is sixty-two times ten to the power five, and the pin has not moved.

Now both numbers count in steps of ten to the power five.

Sixty-two take away eight is fifty-four.

So we have fifty-four times ten to the power five.

Fifty-four is ten or more, so this is not standard form yet.

Fifty-four is five point four times ten, so the power goes up by one, back to six.

The answer is five point four times ten to the power six.

Let's check with ordinary numbers.

Six million two hundred thousand take away eight hundred thousand is five million four hundred thousand.

That is five point four times ten to the power six, so the answer is right.

You can now add or subtract numbers in standard form by first matching their powers of ten.

Rewrite the number with the larger power into the smaller power; the pin does not move.

Then the coefficients count steps of the same size, so you can add or subtract them.

Keep that power of ten.

Then rewrite the answer so the coefficient is between one and ten.

Never add or subtract the indices here: the index laws are only for multiplying and dividing.

And the puzzle: three times ten to the power four, add two times ten to the power five, is two point three times ten to the power five.
