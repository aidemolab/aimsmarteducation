Multiplying and dividing in standard form

Goal: You will learn to multiply or divide numbers in standard form.

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn to multiply or divide two numbers in standard form, and give the answer in standard form.

Here is a puzzle.

What is three times ten to the power four, times two times ten to the power five?

Pick the card you think is right, and hold it.

Writing out all the zeros is slow, and it is easy to drop one.

In standard form, the whole calculation takes a few short steps, often without a calculator.

You already know that you can multiply numbers in any order and get the same answer.

You know that when you multiply powers of ten, you add the indices.

And when you divide powers of ten, you subtract the indices.

Let's write the puzzle as one long multiplication: three, times ten to the power four, times two, times ten to the power five.

Every sign in it is a times sign.

So we can change the order without changing the value.

We move the two next to the three.

That puts the coefficients together and the powers of ten together.

On the scale, multiplying by two times ten to the power five is one big jump.

We can split it into a times two jump and a times ten to the power five jump.

The order of the jumps does not matter, so we land on the same pin.

Three times two is six.

Ten to the power four times ten to the power five is ten to the power nine, because four tens and five more tens make nine tens multiplied.

So the answer is six times ten to the power nine.

Six is between one and ten, so this is already in standard form.

Let's check with ordinary numbers.

Thirty thousand times two hundred thousand is six thousand million.

That is six times ten to the power nine, so that was the right card.

Five times ten to the power nine came from adding the coefficients.

Six times ten to the power twenty came from multiplying the indices.

Six times ten to the power eight lost one power of ten.

So when we multiply, we multiply the coefficients and add the indices.

Now try five times ten to the power three, times four times ten to the power six.

Five times four is twenty.

Three add six is nine.

So we get twenty times ten to the power nine.

But twenty is not less than ten, so this card is not in standard form yet.

Pause point.

Before we fix it, predict the right card.

Is twenty times ten to the power nine the same as:

two times ten to the power eight;

two times ten to the power nine;

two times ten to the power ten;

or twenty times ten to the power ten?

Lock in your choice.

Now we slow down, because this is the key step.

Twenty is two times ten.

If we make the coefficient ten times smaller, the pin moves ten times smaller.

So the power of ten must go up by one, to move the pin back.

Twenty times ten to the power nine is two times ten to the power ten.

The pin stays in the same place; only the card changes.

Dividing works in the same way.

Take eight times ten to the power six, divided by two times ten to the power two.

Dividing by two times ten to the power two means dividing by two, then dividing by ten to the power two.

So we divide the coefficients: eight divided by two is four.

And we subtract the indices: six take away two is four.

The answer is four times ten to the power four, already in standard form.

Second pause point.

Why are we allowed to deal with the coefficients and the powers of ten separately?

Choose the best reason:

because multiplying can be done in any order;

because the numbers are small;

or because the indices are the same.

Hold your answer; we will come back to it in the recap.

Work out two point four times ten to the power minus three, divided by eight times ten to the power four. Give your answer in standard form.

First, we split the division into two parts.

We divide the coefficients, and we divide the powers of ten.

Two point four divided by eight is nought point three.

We know this because twenty-four divided by eight is three, and two point four is ten times smaller than twenty-four.

Next, the powers of ten.

For a division, we subtract the indices: minus three take away four is minus seven.

So we have nought point three times ten to the power minus seven.

But nought point three is less than one, so this is not standard form yet.

We make the coefficient ten times bigger, from nought point three to three.

That alone would make the value ten times bigger.

So the power of ten must go down by one to keep the pin still: from minus seven to minus eight.

The answer is three times ten to the power minus eight.

Three is between one and ten, so it is in standard form.

You can now multiply or divide numbers in standard form.

To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the indices.

To divide, divide the coefficients and subtract the indices.

We can treat the two parts separately because multiplying can be done in any order.

Then check the coefficient.

If it is ten or more, or less than one, rewrite the card so the pin does not move.

And the puzzle: three times ten to the power four, times two times ten to the power five, is six times ten to the power nine.
