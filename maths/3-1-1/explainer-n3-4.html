Transcript

Cancel first, calculate less

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn how to make a calculation easier by cancelling a common factor.

You'll also learn how to tell when cancelling is allowed.

Here's a puzzle.

Two people work out 24 × 15, divided by 12, without a calculator.

One person multiplies first, gets 360, and then divides 360 by 12.

The other person writes 2 × 15 straight away.

Is the second person allowed to do that? Decide, and hold on to your answer.

Part of your AQA GCSE maths exam is taken without a calculator.

Small numbers mean fewer chances to slip.

But a shortcut used in the wrong place gives a wrong answer, so we need to know exactly when it's valid.

In N3.2, you saw that multiplying by 12 and dividing by 12 undo each other.

You also know equivalent fractions, like 2/4 being the same as 1/2.

And you can multiply fractions, from N2.9.

Let's go back to the puzzle.

We can write it as a fraction, with 24 × 15 on top and 12 on the bottom.

24 is 2 × 12, so the top is really 2 × 12 × 15.

The top is multiplied by 12, and the bottom divides by 12.

Those two undo each other, because they're inverse operations.

So we can cancel them, and we're left with 2 × 15, which is 30.

Let's check the long way: 24 × 15 is 360, and 360 ÷ 12 is 30.

Both routes land on the same pin, so the second person was allowed to cancel.

Here's a new term.

A common factor is a number that divides exactly into both the top and the bottom.

Cancelling means dividing the top and the bottom by a common factor.

The value stays the same, because dividing top and bottom by 6 is the same as dividing by 6/6, which is 1.

So 18/24 cancels to 3/4.

Now, the big question: when is cancelling valid?

Pause point. Pause the video now. What is 12 + 5, all over 12? Lock in one card: 5, 6 or 17/12. Then press play.

Let's slow down here, because this is the key step.

The fraction bar groups the whole top, so we work out the top first: 12 + 5 is 17.

So the fraction is 17/12.

That's a bit more than 1, so it can't be 5 or 6.

The 12 on top is only one part of a sum, so it is not a factor of the whole top.

Compare it with 12 × 5, all over 12.

Here the whole top is 12 lots of 5, so dividing by 12 leaves 5.

So the rule is: you can only cancel a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom.

A sum can still be cancelled if the factor goes into every term.

In 12 + 8, all over 4, 4 goes into 12 and into 8.

So the whole top is 4 lots of 3 + 2.

That means it simplifies to 3 + 2, which is 5.

Check: the top is 20, and 20 ÷ 4 is 5.

When you multiply fractions, all the tops multiply together and all the bottoms multiply together.

So you can cancel any top with any bottom.

In 3/4 × 8/9, 3 and 9 share a factor of 3, and 4 and 8 share a factor of 4.

That leaves 1/1 × 2/3, which is 2/3.

Check: 3 × 8 is 24, 4 × 9 is 36, and 24/36 is 2/3.

Without a calculator, let's work out 36 × 35, divided by 14 × 9.

Step one: look for a number on top and a number on the bottom that share a factor.

36 and 9 share a factor of 9, but they aren't directly above each other.

Pause point. Pause the video now. Can we cancel 36 with 9? Choose yes or no, and justify your choice. Then press play.

Yes, we can.

That's because the top and the bottom are both products, with no plus or minus signs.

So a factor of 36 is a factor of the whole top, and a factor of 9 is a factor of the whole bottom.

Step two: cancel the 9.

36 ÷ 9 is 4, and 9 ÷ 9 is 1.

Step three: 35 and 14 share a factor of 7, for the same reason.

35 ÷ 7 is 5, and 14 ÷ 7 is 2.

Step four: multiply what's left.

The top is 4 × 5, which is 20.

The bottom is 2 × 1, which is 2.

And 20 ÷ 2 is 10.

Step five: check the long way.

36 × 35 is 1260, and 14 × 9 is 126.

1260 ÷ 126 is 10, so both routes land on the same pin.

You've learned to simplify a calculation by cancelling a common factor, only where it's valid.

Cancelling means dividing the whole top and the whole bottom by the same number, so the value doesn't change.

It's valid when the factor divides the whole top and the whole bottom.

You can't cancel just one term of a sum.
