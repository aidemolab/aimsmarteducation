Transcript

Run it backwards: inverse operations

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn how to run a calculation backwards.

That tells you where it started, and whether an answer is right.

Here's a puzzle.

Two people work out 156 ÷ 12.

One person gets 13.

The other gets 14.

How could you find out who's right, without doing the division again? Hold on to your idea.

In an exam, you can't ask anyone if your answer is right.

But you can check it yourself, by running the calculation backwards.

Later in the course, solving an equation is exactly this: undoing steps.

You already know the four operations from N2.

You know that adding moves a pin to the right on the number line.

And subtracting moves it to the left.

You also know that 7 × 4 is 28.

Here is an operation arrow: times 4, from 7 to 28.

An inverse operation is the operation that undoes it.

It brings you back to the pin you started on.

Let's run the times 4 arrow backwards.

Divide by 4 takes 28 back to 7.

That works because multiplying by 4 made four equal groups, and dividing by 4 splits them back into one group.

Adding and subtracting are inverses too.

Plus 6 moves the pin 6 to the right, so −2 + 6 is 4.

Minus 6 moves it 6 back to the left, so 4 − 6 is −2.

We're back where we started.

For a positive number, squaring and square-rooting are inverses as well.

5² is 25, and √25 takes you back to 5.

Now we can solve the puzzle.

If divide by 12 takes 156 to the answer, then times 12 must take the answer back to 156.

13 × 12 is 156, so 13 is right.

14 × 12 is 168, which is not 156, so 14 is wrong.

This works with negative numbers too.

Say you work out −5 − 3 and get −8.

To check, add 3 back on: −8 + 3 is −5.

That's where we started, so −8 is right.

If someone wrote 8, the check would give 8 + 3, which is 11, not −5.

So we'd know 8 was wrong.

It works with fractions too.

In N2 you found that 3/4 ÷ 1/8 is 6, because six eighths fit into three quarters.

To check, multiply back: 6 × 1/8 is 6/8.

6/8 simplifies to 3/4, so the answer is right.

What if there are two steps?

Here's a machine: it adds 2, then multiplies by 5, and the output is 35.

Pause point. Pause the video now. To find the input, which undo step comes first: take away 2, or divide by 5? Lock in one card. Then press play.

Let's slow down here, because this is the key step.

The last step was times 5.

The output came straight out of that step, so it's the first step we can reach.

So we undo it first: 35 ÷ 5 is 7.

Then we undo the plus 2: 7 − 2 is 5.

So the input was 5.

Check it forwards: 5 + 2 is 7, and 7 × 5 is 35.

If you took away 2 first, you'd get 33 ÷ 5, which is 6.6.

Run 6.6 forwards: 6.6 + 2 is 8.6, and 8.6 × 5 is 43, not 35.

It's like putting on socks, then shoes: to undo it, the shoes come off first.

I think of a number.

I multiply it by 3, then subtract 5.

My answer is 16.

What was my number?

Step one: swap each step for its inverse.

Times 3 is undone by divide by 3.

Minus 5 is undone by plus 5.

Pause point. Pause the video now. Which inverse do we use first, plus 5 or divide by 3? Justify your choice. Then press play.

Step two: we add 5 first.

That's because subtracting 5 was the last thing done, so it's the first thing to undo.

16 + 5 is 21.

Step three: undo the times 3.

21 ÷ 3 is 7.

Step four: check, by running the machine forwards from 7.

7 × 3 is 21, and 21 − 5 is 16.

That's the output we were given, so my number was 7.

What if we undo in the wrong order, dividing by 3 first and then adding 5?

We'd get 16 ÷ 3, which is 16 thirds, and then add 5 to get 31 thirds.

Run that forwards: times 3 gives 31, and minus 5 gives 26, not 16.

The check catches the mistake.

You've learned to use inverse operations to undo a calculation and to check an answer.

Each operation has an inverse that brings you back to the starting pin.

To undo several steps, undo the last step first.

To check an answer, run it backwards and see if you land where you began.

1. A machine adds 2, then multiplies by 5. The output is 35. To find the input, which undo step comes first?

Pause point 2 · Multiple choice

2. I multiply a number by 3, then subtract 5, and get 16. Which inverse do we use first?
