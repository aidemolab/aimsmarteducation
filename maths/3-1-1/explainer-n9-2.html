Reading a calculator's standard-form display

Goal: You will learn to interpret a calculator's standard-form display.

In this video you'll learn one thing.

You'll learn to read a standard-form display on a calculator, and say what number it means.

Here is a puzzle.

You work out nought point nought nought six times nought point nought nought seven on a calculator.

The screen shows four point two, then a capital E, then minus nought five.

Your friend says it means four point two to the power minus five.

Do you agree?

Or do you think it means four point two times ten to the power minus five?

Pick a card and hold it.

A calculator shows very big and very small answers in standard form, because all the digits will not fit.

If you misread the display, a correct calculation still gives a wrong answer.

You already know that standard form is A times ten to the power n.

You know how to turn it into an ordinary number by moving the digits.

And you know that a negative power of ten gives a small positive number.

Now we slow down, because this is the key step.

On the display, the letter E stands for times ten to the power.

The power belongs to the ten, not to the four point two.

So four point two E minus nought five means four point two times ten to the power minus five.

The nought in front of the five only fills a space on the screen.

So minus nought five is just minus five.

Some calculators show a small times ten instead of the letter E.

It means exactly the same thing.

Pause point.

Before we work it out, predict where this pin goes.

Is four point two times ten to the power minus five:

nought point nought nought nought nought four two;

nought point nought nought four two;

minus four hundred and twenty thousand;

or a number bigger than one?

Lock in your choice.

The power is minus five.

So we move the digits of four point two five columns right.

That gives nought point nought nought nought nought four two.

The pin is just to the right of zero.

It is a small positive number.

So the right card in our puzzle was four point two times ten to the power minus five.

We can check that size without the calculator.

Six times seven is forty-two.

Each number has three decimal places, so the answer has six decimal places.

That gives nought point nought nought nought nought four two, which matches the display.

Second pause point.

A classmate copies the display into their answer exactly as it looks, with the letter E.

Is that a good final answer?

Choose yes or no, and think why.

No.

E is the calculator's short code, not maths notation.

In an exam, write A times ten to the power n, or write the ordinary number.

You work out nought point nought nought nought six divided by twenty-five thousand on a calculator. The display shows two point four E minus nought eight. Write the answer in standard form and as an ordinary number, and check its size.

First, we read the E as times ten to the power.

So the display means two point four times ten to the power minus eight.

That is the answer in standard form.

Next, the ordinary number.

The power is minus eight, so we move the digits of two point four eight columns right.

That gives nought point nought nought nought nought nought nought nought two four.

There are seven noughts after the decimal point, then two four.

Last, we check the size.

Nought point nought nought nought six is six times ten to the power minus four.

Twenty-five thousand is two point five times ten to the power four.

Six divided by two point five is two point four.

Dividing by ten to the power four takes four off the power, so minus four becomes minus eight.

So the answer is two point four times ten to the power minus eight, which matches the display.

You can now interpret a calculator's standard-form display.

The letter E means times ten to the power.

So two point four E minus nought eight is two point four times ten to the power minus eight.

That is a small positive number.

Never read the display as a power of the first number.

Write your final answer in proper standard form or as an ordinary number.

Then check its size with a rough calculation.
