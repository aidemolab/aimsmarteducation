3.1.1 Structure and calculation · GCSE Maths · AQA 8300 · N1–N9

Part 7 of 8

Recap

The key ideas of the unit on one page, with a confidence check and help links for every N point.

What you learned

In this unit you learned to compare and order numbers, choose and carry out the right operation, and tell whether a step has changed a value or only rewritten it.

  • N1: You can order negative numbers, decimals and fractions by their position on one number line, and choose the symbol (=, ≠, <, >, ≤, ≥) that makes a statement true.
  • N2: You can add, subtract, multiply and divide integers, decimals, fractions and mixed numbers, positive and negative, use place value, and pick the right calculation for money words such as profit, debit, credit and VAT.
  • N3: You can decide which step comes first (brackets, powers, roots, then × and ÷, then + and −), undo a calculation with inverse operations, find a reciprocal and cancel only where it is valid.
  • N4: You can tell factors from multiples, spot primes, write a number as a product of primes in index form, and use the HCF for "largest equal groups" and the LCM for "when do the cycles meet".
  • N5: You can list outcomes systematically by holding one choice fixed and show that none is missing or counted twice; at Higher tier you can count choices with the product rule.
  • N6: You can work out powers as repeated multiplication, recognise key powers and square numbers, find roots by reversing a power, and at Higher tier estimate a root or power between known values.
  • N7: You can calculate with roots and with integer indices, including zero and negative indices, and at Higher tier read a fractional index as a root and a power.
  • N8: You can keep fraction and π answers exact, and at Higher tier simplify surds, collect like surds and rationalise denominators.
  • N9: You can convert to and from standard form, read a calculator display, and multiply, divide, add and subtract numbers in standard form.
