Q7 · Numeric answer (shown as multiple choice: the answer is in standard form) · Non-calculator · [2 marks]

Work out 3.6 × 10⁵ + 8 × 10⁴. Give your answer in standard form.

Mark scheme

M1 for matching the powers, for example 36 × 10⁴ + 8 × 10⁴, or for 360 000 + 80 000 seen.

A1 for 4.4 × 10⁵.

Q8(a) · Spot the error in a worked solution (explanation shown as multiple choice) · Non-calculator · [1 mark]

Jo works out 5 × 10⁻² − 3 × 10⁻³ like this: “5 − 3 = 2 and −2 − (−3) = 1, so the answer is 2 × 10¹.” (a) Explain the mistake Jo has made.

Mark scheme

B1 for a correct explanation, for example “the powers must be the same before you subtract the numbers” or “you do not subtract the indices when subtracting”.

Q8(b) · Spot the error in a worked solution · Non-calculator · [2 marks]

(b) Work out the correct answer to 5 × 10⁻² − 3 × 10⁻³. Give your answer in standard form.

Mark scheme

M1 for matching the powers, for example 50 × 10⁻³ − 3 × 10⁻³, or for 0.05 − 0.003 seen.

A1 for 4.7 × 10⁻².

Q9 · Open-ended (shown as multiple choice: pick the answer that earns the marks) · Non-calculator · [3 marks]

A craft shop sells a bag of 2.5 × 10⁴ beads. Each bead has a mass of 4 × 10⁻² grams. The empty bag has a mass of 1.5 × 10² grams. Sam says, “The full bag has a mass of more than 1 kilogram.” Is Sam correct? You must show your working.

Mark scheme

M1 for the mass of the beads, (2.5 × 10⁴) × (4 × 10⁻²), or 1000 g, or 1 × 10³ g seen.

M1 for adding the bag, 1000 + 150, or 1 × 10³ + 1.5 × 10², with the powers matched.

A1 for 1150 g or 1.15 × 10³ g and a correct conclusion (“Yes”, because 1150 g > 1000 g). A bare “Yes” with no working scores 0.

Example answer

Example (a fixed example, not marked by a computer): Beads: (2.5 × 10⁴) × (4 × 10⁻²) = (2.5 × 4) × (10⁴ × 10⁻²) = 10 × 10² = 1 × 10³ g. Bag: 1.5 × 10² g. Total: 10 × 10² + 1.5 × 10² = 11.5 × 10² = 1.15 × 10³ g = 1150 g. 1 kg = 1000 g, and 1150 g > 1000 g, so Sam is correct.
